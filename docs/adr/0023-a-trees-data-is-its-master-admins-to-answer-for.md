# 0023 A Tree's Data Is Its Master Admin's to Answer For

Members and Persons speak only with their Master Admin, never the Owner (LIN-85). The Master Admin and Admins can export a whole Tree as GEDCOM at any time, and Members can't bulk export. A Person asking to be removed asks the Master Admin. If the Master Admin ignores them, that stays between the two of them.

Deleting an account removes the account and its memberships, and its Person stays on the Tree, unbound. A Master Admin must hand over or delete their Tree first. Only a Master Admin can delete a Tree, with 30 days to recover before it is permanent.

If a Master Admin has not signed in for 90 days, an Admin can take the role, after the Master Admin is notified by email. A Tree with no Admins stays as it is, and its Members keep using it.

The Owner keeps one contact address, listed only in the terms and `security.txt`, for legal notices and security reports and not for support. The terms and privacy policy are written in-house from a standard template and accepted at sign-up. They say the Owner is not liable for anything and that use of a Tree is entirely up to the user. They also say an ignored removal request is between the person and the Master Admin. Accounts are for ages 18 and up, and children can be Persons but not account holders.

## Considered Options

- **The Owner steps in when a Master Admin doesn't respond.** Rejected: the Owner does not want to deal with Members, and ADR 0020 keeps the Owner out of other Trees.
- **Legal research and lawyer review.** Rejected by the Owner.

## Consequences

- Terms can only limit liability as far as the law allows, so the Owner keeps a host's duties for sensitive family data (ADR 0017) and carries the risk of having no legal review.
- A legal demand can still reach the Owner through the contact address.
- A Tree whose Master Admin has gone and which has no Admins can never be managed again.
- Sending the 90-day notice means Osra must be able to send email.
